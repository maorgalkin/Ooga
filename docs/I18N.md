# Languages (English and Hebrew)

Ooga is in English and Hebrew. Every on-screen string lives in the translation files, and CI keeps it that way (`npm run i18n:untranslated`, the `UNTRANSLATED_MAX` ratchet at 0).

## How it fits together

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| `src/i18n/index.ts` | Sets up i18next with both languages; `applyLanguage()` switches language and page direction (`<html lang dir>`) and remembers the choice in this browser |
| `src/i18n/locales/en.json`, `he.json` | The text, nested by feature (`language.settings.title`) |
| `src/i18n/locales/guide.en.json`, `guide.he.json` | The Getting started guide's words, a namespace of their own (`useTranslation('guide')`); `localizeGuide()` in `src/content/guide.ts` puts them together with the guide's shape |
| `src/services/preferencesService.ts` | Reads and saves `language` and `address_as` in the auth user's metadata, next to their name, so they follow the account to every device |
| `src/i18n/LanguageSync.tsx` | Applies the signed-in account's language; on first sign-in, carries over a language picked while signed out |
| `src/hooks/useT.ts` | `t` that picks the reader's form of address (below) |
| `src/components/settings/LanguageSettings.tsx` | The Language card in Budget settings |
| The switch in `PublicLayout` | Lets signed-out visitors change language on About and the Guide |

Which language shows: the account's choice, else this browser's last choice, else English. English stays the default even for Hebrew browsers: `HebrewOffer` (`src/i18n/HebrewOffer.tsx`) offers them Hebrew once, in Hebrew, and either answer is remembered (Hebrew on the account when signed in; "keep English" on this browser only).

## Writing translatable text

- Use `useT()` in anything a signed-in person sees, `useTranslation().t` on signed-out pages. Keys are English-free identifiers grouped by feature; add every key to both files (a test fails if `he.json` is missing one).
- Money goes through `formatCurrency*` and dates through `getUserLocale()`: both follow the language (`he-IL` on Hebrew screens). A hard-coded `'en-US'` stays English on Hebrew screens.
- Text from banks is already Hebrew: never translate it; render it with `dir="auto"`.

## Addressing the reader in Hebrew

Hebrew verbs and adjectives change with the reader's gender. People can choose how Ooga addresses them (Budget settings → Language → "How Hebrew addresses you"): neutral, female or male. It's stored as `address_as` in their account and is optional.

- **Every key has a neutral version.** Write it with verbal nouns and impersonal forms (`הוספת הוצאה`, `שמירה`, `כדאי לנסות שוב`). It's what signed-out pages, the guide, household-wide text and anyone who chose neutral see.
- **Add gendered variants only where a sentence reads better gendered:** `key_female` and `key_male` next to `key`, in `he.json` only. `useT()` picks the variant for the reader's choice and falls back to the neutral key when there's none.
- Buttons stay verbal nouns (`שמירה`, not `שמור`/`שמרי`), as Israeli apps usually do.

## Right to left

`applyLanguage('he')` sets `dir="rtl"` on `<html>`, and the layout mirrors with it. That works because classes name sides logically, which behave exactly like left/right in English:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| `ml-`/`mr-`, `pl-`/`pr-` | `ms-`/`me-`, `ps-`/`pe-` |
| `left-`/`right-` | `start-`/`end-` |
| `text-left`/`text-right` | `text-start`/`text-end` (on each `<th>`: a header cell ignores an inherited `text-start` and centres itself) |
| `rounded-l`/`rounded-r`, `border-l`/`border-r` | `rounded-s`/`rounded-e`, `border-s`/`border-e` |
| `space-x-2` | `space-x-2 rtl:space-x-reverse` |

`tests/i18n/rtlClasses.test.ts` fails on a physical class, a `space-x` without its reverse, or an unmirrored arrow.

- **Arrows and chevrons** that point along the text (back, next, open) get `rtl:-scale-x-100`. Not ones turned to point down with `rotate-90`, which would end up pointing up.
- **Movement CSS can't mirror** (framer-motion `x`, drag offsets, `translate-x` on toggle knobs): flip the sign with `isRtl()` from `src/i18n`, or add `rtl:-translate-x-…`.
- **Charts stay left to right**, as in most Israeli finance apps: `.recharts-wrapper` is forced LTR in `index.css`, and the Insights column chart has `dir="ltr"`. Hebrew labels inside them still read correctly.
- **Kept physical on purpose:** `left-1/2 -translate-x-1/2` centring (symmetric anyway), and `GuideClip`, whose pointers sit on fixed screenshots.
- **To look at it:** switch with the language button in the public top bar or Budget settings → Language; `node scripts/demo/dev-against-test.mjs --preview` serves the demo household.

## Outside the app

- **New households:** a sign-up in Hebrew saves `language: 'he'` with the account, and the database names the household "משק הבית של …" (migration 040). Existing households keep their names.
- **Sign-in emails:** Supabase's templates carry both languages and choose by the saved language; they live in the dashboard, with the source in `docs/auth-emails/` (`docs/AUTH_EMAILS.md`).
